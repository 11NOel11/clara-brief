1. Overview

Clara helps a couple understand a premarital genetic screening result. Each spouse gets a private explanation on their own phone, in Arabic or English. A genetics specialist decides when the result is released, after a counselling session, and Clara then helps the couple choose next steps and sends a referral. Every couple, result and clinician in the prototype is synthetic.

2. The problem

  • Abu Dhabi's premarital screening now includes a genetic test. The result is about both spouses at once, and it is easy to misread.
  • Whoever hears the result first is left explaining it to the other.
  • "Carrier" does not mean "ill". A carrier is usually healthy; it matters when both spouses carry a change in the same gene, which means a 1 in 4 chance in each pregnancy. That is a chance, not a diagnosis.

We set two rules: the software never shows one spouse something about the other that they have not agreed to share, and a person, never a model, stands behind every decision and every sentence.

3. How Clara works

  1. The lab reports a result. Clara records it. An at-risk result is held for the specialist, and the phones receive nothing new.
  2. The specialist releases it. Only the specialist can release a held result, and only once a counselling session exists. If any other seat tries, the rules refuse and nothing changes.
  3. Each spouse reads their own explanation. A spouse's phone receives only their own view. A carrier finding reaches the other spouse only if its owner chooses to share it.
  4. Each spouse checks their understanding. A short comprehension check, answered in their own words, stays private to that spouse.
  5. The couple choose what happens next. Options are shown unranked and in a shuffled order. Declining is a full option. Questions about cost, cover or faith go to a person.
  6. A referral is sent and tracked. It carries only what the receiver needs and is tracked from sent to received, accepted, booked, attended and closed. In pregnancy it is marked urgent.
  7. The programme office sees only counts. It sees how many couples understood, booked, were seen in time and were referred, with privacy noise added, and never what a couple chose.

Relatives: Clara never names or stores a relative. Each spouse chooses for their own side: tell no one, pass on a letter, or ask a counsellor to make contact, which needs a completed comprehension check and a 72-hour cooling-off period.

5. Privacy and security

  • Each spouse's view is built on the server. A field a spouse may not see is never copied into their view, so it never reaches their phone. A property test builds 2,000 random cases and fails if any view holds a field its role may not see.
  • Staff seats differ in the prototype. The specialist, family doctor and lab seats receive the case and build their view on the device. That is acceptable for synthetic cases; a real deployment would build staff views on the server and add clinician sign-in.
  • The audit record cannot be edited quietly. Each entry holds the previous entry's hash, so any edit breaks the chain. All 2,000 random one-byte edits in the test were caught.
  • Seats cannot impersonate each other. Each request carries a token signed with HMAC-SHA256 (64 bits), checked in constant time. Rooms are deleted after 3 hours idle or 12 hours at most.
  • Programme counts hide any single couple. Counts get calibrated random noise (Laplace, 95% of it within ±10), and groups under 10 are withheld.
  • Little leaves the clinic. A calendar entry says only "Appointment". The referral carries no gene, no answers and nothing about relatives. Cloudflare request logging and metrics are off.

6. How AI is used

Clara works without a model. Models help with seven narrow tasks; every answer is checked, and anything doubtful goes to a person. A model never writes the words a family reads and never decides a step of a case.

TaskWhat the model doesWhat it never does
Ask ClaraPicks one approved answerWrite an answer
ComprehensionReads a spouse's own wordsShow them to the partner
Lab intakeCopies six fields from a reportInvent a field
Care agentProposes the next stepRelease a result
Session briefDrafts notes for the specialist–
CoachWrites rehearsal notes for counsellors–
Arabic checkFlags drafts for a reviewerApprove a sentence

For Ask Clara, rules run first: questions about cost, cover or faith always go to a person, and medical-detail and family questions do when no approved answer fits. The model then picks one topic from the allowed menu, and the answer is used only if the topic is on the menu and the model's confidence is at least 0.6. Otherwise the spouse is offered a counsellor, who sees a heading such as "Cost and cover", never the words typed.

Models: K2 Think Horizon (MBZUAI) first, OpenAI as the fallback. Every task has a rule-based fallback, and one setting turns AI off for everyone.

9. Limitations

  • Synthetic data and mocked partners only; all 613 sentences are unsigned; thresholds are not yet agreed with the Department of Health.
  • The AI result is on the questions the rules were built from; the fine-tuned model has no results yet.
  • Whether Clara improves understanding is untested until the study runs after ethics approval.
  • No Malaffi conformance test, patient record, identity provider or clinician sign-in; staff seats in a room hold the case.
  • No load test at programme scale and no outside security review of the room protocol.
  • Not a medical device; not cleared for clinical use.
